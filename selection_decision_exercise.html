8. Why BLUP is different

In a real breeding program, EBVs are not normally calculated by an arbitrary weighted average of “own BV” and parental EBV. Instead, a mixed model can use many sources of information simultaneously.

y = Xb + Zu + e
  • y = observed records
  • b = fixed effects such as contemporary group/pen
  • u = additive genetic effects to be predicted
  • e = residual effects

Key idea: BLUP predicts genetic merit using the animal's record and the information contained in relatives, pedigree, repeated records, progeny and other connected records.

Take-home: phenotype → adjustment for systematic effects → genetic prediction → accuracy → selection decision.

Model Answers:

1. Why did the top raw performer look best initially?
This animal had the highest absolute observed phenotype (raw performance). Looking only at the raw data ignores the environmental advantage. The animal happened to be raised in the contemporary group that had the most favourable environmental conditions, which artificially inflated its raw phenotype.

2. What assumption is being made when we subtract a contemporary group effect?
We assume that the environmental conditions affect all animals within that specific group equally. We assume there is no Genotype by Environment interaction occurring, and that genetics are distributed somewhat randomly across the groups (meaning the group mean truly reflects the environment).

3. Why is the own-record EBV smaller than the phenotypic deviation?
The phenotypic deviation from the mean includes variance from both additive genetics and the environment. Because heritability is less than 1.0, only a proportion of that observed superiority is actually due to additive genetic effects that can be passed on to progeny. Multiplying by heritability "shrinks" the estimate back toward the population mean.

4. Why might an animal with a slightly lower EBV be preferable if its accuracy is much higher?
Selection decisions involve risk management. A low-accuracy EBV has a wide confidence interval, meaning the true breeding value could end up being significantly lower than the estimate once progeny are born. An animal with high accuracy provides certainty.

5. What additional information would BLUP use?
Pedigree records, progeny records, repeated records, correlated traits, and simultaneous fixed effects (solving for environmental and genetic effects at the same time).

6. How would genomic information change this exercise for young animals?
It would dramatically increase the accuracy of the EBV for young, unproven animals. Instead of relying purely on an own-record and a parent-average, genomic testing analyses the actual SNPs to determine exactly which alleles were inherited, allowing differentiation between full siblings at birth.

7. What happens if the two best sires are closely related to the rest of the breeding population?
Selecting them will rapidly increase the rate of inbreeding within the herd or flock. This increases the risk of inbreeding depression and the chance of expressing deleterious recessive mutations. This illustrates the need for tactical mate selection and Optimal Contribution Selection (OCS).
